A first look at
fixed points

How repeatedly doing the same thing
can lead somewhere new.

A short introduction · Analysis · 4 min read
01 THE IDEA

Imagine a process that brings any two points closer together. Apply it again and again. Each step gets smaller, and the sequence begins to settle. A fixed point is where the process stops changing anything.

T(x∗) = x∗(1)

Think of a fixed point as an answer that survives its own rule.

02 A PRECISE STATEMENT
THE CONTRACTION PRINCIPLE

Let (X, d) be a nonempty complete metric space. Suppose T : X → X satisfies

d(Tx, Ty) ≤ q · d(x, y),   0 ≤ q < 1.

Then T has a unique fixed point. Starting from any x₀ ∈ X, the iterates xₙ₊₁ = T(xₙ) converge to that point.

03 WHY IT WORKS

Each application of T shrinks the distance by a factor of at most q. After n steps, consecutive iterates are at most qⁿ d(x₁, x₀) apart. We need a little more than shrinking steps: we need the entire remaining journey to become small.

d(xₘ, xₙ) ≤ qⁿ1 − q d(x₁, x₀),   m > n.(2)

The geometric tail tends to zero, so the iterates form a Cauchy sequence. Completeness ensures its limit x* stays inside X. Since T is continuous, passing to the limit gives T(x*) = x*. Finally, two fixed points would satisfy d(x*, y*) ≤ q · d(x*, y*), forcing them to be the same.
